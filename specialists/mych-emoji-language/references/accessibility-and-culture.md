# Accessibility and Cultural Meaning

Using emoji so that everyone understands the message as intended.

## Accessibility

- Screen readers announce emoji by their Unicode names (often the CLDR short names), so "🔥🔥🔥" may be read as "fire fire fire". Use emoji sparingly and never as the only carrier of meaning.
- Do not replace words with emoji in instructions, labels or error messages.
- Give icon-only buttons accessible names; give emoji used as images text alternatives.
- Avoid animated emoji that flash or loop endlessly; respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Color alone (for example red versus green circles for status) fails for many users; add text or distinct shapes.

## Cultural meaning

- Gestures differ: some hand gestures are positive in one culture and offensive in another.
- Objects, animals, food and colors carry different associations across regions and generations.
- Platform designs differ, which changes tone: test the actual rendering your audience sees.
- Skin tones and gender variants support representation; offer them rather than defaulting everyone to one appearance, and avoid assigning them to people without their choice.

## In product communication

- Match the brand's tone and the moment: emoji can soften casual messages but undermine serious ones (security incidents, billing problems, apologies).
- Keep a short, reviewed list of approved emoji for official communication, with their intended meanings.
- Localize: review emoji choices for each market as you would review copy.
